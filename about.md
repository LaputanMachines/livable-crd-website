<p>
  <strong>Livable CRD</strong> is a non-partisan voter-education project focused on
  municipal elections in the Capital Regional District of British Columbia. We are
  not a political party and we do not endorse candidates. We publish information
  so residents can make informed choices.
</p>

<h2>Our mission</h2>
<p>
  Municipal councils decide the things people notice first: what gets built and
  where, how often the bus comes, whether a street is safe to walk or cycle
  down, what the region does about emissions, and what happens to the parks,
  libraries and services everyone shares. They are also the elections with the
  fewest voters and the smallest margins. Turnout in B.C. municipal elections
  runs at roughly one in three, so a council seat can turn on a few dozen
  ballots.
</p>
<p>
  Finding out where a candidate actually stands is the hard part. Most of these
  races have no party platform to read, local coverage is thin, and an
  all-candidates meeting is one evening on one night that most people cannot
  make. So we ask instead. Every confirmed candidate in the region gets
  <a href="{{ '/questionnaire/' | relative_url }}">the same questionnaire</a>,
  written by the organizations that work in each policy area, and we publish
  what comes back on <a href="{{ '/scorecard/' | relative_url }}">one
  scorecard</a> before the vote: answers, grades, and the candidates who did
  not reply. Our aim is that any resident can spend a few minutes here and go to the
  polls knowing what they are choosing between.
</p>

<h2>How the coalition works</h2>
<p>
  Participating organizations collaborate on a shared questionnaire covering topics
  across ten topic areas, from transit and housing to arts, climate, reconciliation, walking, cycling, healthcare, governance, and general livability. Responses are collected from
  candidates (or documented when candidates decline to respond). Each participating
  group submits questions in its area of focus and grades candidates' responses to
  the questions it submitted, using a published methodology so the process is
  transparent and reproducible.
</p>
