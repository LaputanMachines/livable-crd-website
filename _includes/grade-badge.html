Two glyphs were tried and both said the wrong thing. A text ellipsis sat low in the chip and read as a broken em dash, the one mark it has to be distinguished from. A clockwise circular arrow replaced it and read as Reload, because that is what that arrow means everywhere else on the web — an instruction to click, on a chip that is not a control. A horizontal ellipsis fails the same way: three dots in a row is the overflow-menu icon. Filled rather than stroked, unlike the topic icons and the speech bubble. This is the smallest thing on the site, about 10px across in a phone-width matrix cell, and an outline turns to mush at that size. A silhouette keeps its shape. Drawn as sand rather than as glass: a full funnel at the top, a stream, and a pile at the bottom, held between two rounded caps. An outlined glass with sand inside it is the more literal picture and was rejected on size — the outline and the sand end up a pixel apart in a matrix cell and merge into a blob. Mass survives where line weight does not, and two caps over a funnel and a pile are enough to read as an hourglass without drawing the glass at all. Two earlier attempts failed on the same axis. Two triangles meeting at a point have a neck of zero width, which disappears below about 20px and leaves a bowtie; squat funnels read as a bowtie however they are capped. Everything is drawn heavy, and heavier than looked right in isolation. The caps are 4.6 units deep on a 24 grid and fully rounded, the funnel spans very nearly the full width of the cap above it, and the whole middle - the funnel's neck and the stream below it - is 3.2 wide. The funnel stops at that neck rather than tapering to a point: a shape that narrows to nothing in the middle looks pinched however heavy its ends are. Slimmer versions of exactly this shape failed twice over. Thin caps do not look like they could hold anything, and a narrow funnel over a small pile is a stem over a base, which is a goblet. It also has to sit beside the speech bubble, stroked at 3.5, and matching that weight is what keeps the two reading as one set rather than as a bold icon next to a delicate one.
