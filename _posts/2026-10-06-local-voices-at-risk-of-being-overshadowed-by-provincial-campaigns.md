**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

**Victoria, BC, October 6, 2026**. With a provincial election now scheduled just
one week after municipal election day, local volunteer-led project Livable CRD is
urging voters not to lose sight of local issues. Its new
[Candidate Scorecard]({{ '/scorecard/' | relative_url }}) brings together
candidates' responses to key municipal issues on a single website so voters can
do their own research ahead of election day.

## Why we created the Candidate Scorecard

"Municipal elections are where many of the decisions that shape our everyday
lives are made, and are one of the places your vote can have the biggest impact.
Our volunteers care deeply about local issues, and we want to make sure local
candidates and local voices don't get lost in the noise of a provincial
election," says Ray Oakley, a Livable CRD volunteer.

The Candidate Scorecard covers a broad range of topics ranging from governance to
arts and culture, and received over 80 responses from candidates from 12
municipalities across the CRD. The project was developed through the efforts of
local volunteers and nonprofit organizations who care deeply about their
communities and came together to create a practical resource for voters ahead of
the municipal election.

With candidates running across municipalities throughout the region, finding
information can require visiting multiple websites, searching through campaign
platforms, and navigating different approaches to communicating policy positions.
The Candidate Scorecard provides a centralized starting point for voters who want
to learn more.

"Our volunteers have spent months working on the Scorecard because we believe
municipal elections deserve attention. Having two elections back to back makes
our work more important than ever," says Michael Bassili, Livable CRD volunteer.
"We're not asking people to choose between paying attention to provincial or
municipal politics, we're trying to make sure they have the resources to
understand their local choices too."

Livable CRD encourages voters to use the scorecard as one resource in their own
research and to explore candidates' positions before making their own decisions.

## How the scorecard works

Livable CRD does not endorse candidates. Each response was assessed against a
published, reproducible methodology and assigned a letter grade; where a
candidate did not respond, that is noted on their profile as well, so residents
can see the full picture either way.

Participating organizations collaborated on a shared questionnaire, with each
group contributing questions in its area of expertise. Livable CRD sent that
questionnaire to every candidate it could identify running in this municipal
election, and graded the responses using a published, reproducible methodology
and a clear letter-grade system. Results are organized by municipality, and each
candidate's profile includes both their letter grade and their full response, so
residents can dig in as deep as they want.

## About Livable CRD

Livable CRD is a non-partisan coalition of community groups preparing a candidate
scorecard for municipal elections across the Capital Regional District of British
Columbia. Our mission is to make voting easier by providing clear, accessible
information that empowers more people to participate in local elections. The
coalition informs; it does not endorse. Learn more at
[livablecrd.ca]({{ '/' | relative_url }}) or read more
